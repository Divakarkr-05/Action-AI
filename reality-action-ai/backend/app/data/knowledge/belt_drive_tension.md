# Belt Drive Tensioning, Pulley Alignment & Wear Criteria

## Pulley Alignment Types
Belt life and power transmission efficiency depend directly on precise pulley alignment. Three primary misalignment conditions exist:
1. Angular Misalignment (horizontal or vertical): Shafts are not parallel, creating uneven tensile stress across the belt width.
2. Parallel Offset Misalignment: Shafts are parallel but pulleys do not lie in the same coplanar plane.
3. Allowable tolerance: Maximum angular misalignment should not exceed 0.5 degrees (approx 1/16 inch per foot of center distance).

## Alignment Verification Tools
- Straight Edge or String Line: Position across outer machined rim of both driver and driven pulleys to detect 4-point contact.
- Laser Pulley Alignment Tool: Emits a target laser line across sheave grooves to immediately identify angular pitch and parallel offset simultaneously.

## Belt Tensioning Rule of Thumb
- The ideal tension is the lowest tension at which the belt does not slip under peak load conditions.
- Deflection Method:
  1. Measure span length (t) in inches between tangent points of the two pulleys.
  2. Deflection distance = Span length / 64 (approximately 1/64 inch deflection per 1 inch of span length).
  3. Apply recommended deflection force (measured with a spring scale or mechanical tension gauge) at the center of the span.
  4. Compare measured deflection force against manufacturer specs for the specific belt cross-section (A, B, C, 3V, 5V, or synchronous tooth profile).

## Wear and Replacement Criteria
- Transverse cracking across inner belt ribs or teeth: If cracks appear closer than 1/2 inch apart or more than 3 cracks per inch exist, replace immediately.
- Fraying, cord separation, or glazed sidewalls: Indicates slippage, severe heat degradation, or pulley groove wear.
- Sheave groove inspection: Check pulley grooves with a sheave wear gauge. If groove sidewall wear exceeds 1/32 inch, pulleys must be replaced alongside the new belt.
- Re-tension break-in period: Run new belts for 24 hours under normal load, then re-check and adjust tension to compensate for initial seated stretch.
